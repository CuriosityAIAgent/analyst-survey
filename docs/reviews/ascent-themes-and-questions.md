# The Ascent: themes and questions for Monday

For Adam and Eduardo · Monday 28 September 2026 · a five-minute read, plus an appendix for the build team

Nothing here has been tested with respondents. All timings are estimates.

## After the survey, we will be able to tell you

- **What made our Advisors (Goal 1).** Which experiences built them, which traits they brought rather than learned, and how much was the luck of their team.
- **How to modernise A2A (Goal 2).** What Analysts should do more of, less of, and hand to AI. What to keep. What every Analyst should be guaranteed.
- **How to get there faster (Goal 3).** Whether graduates back a shorter path, what can be sped up, what only comes with time, and what "ready" should mean.
- **What the move to HNW needs.** Which skills an Advisor with a bigger book of smaller clients needs that today's programme doesn't build.

We ask the classes of 2022 to 2025: 18 questions on a phone (six to eight minutes, to be timed), all 25 on desktop.

## Why some questions took a minute

The pause came from climbing words: people had to decode "kit", "pitch", "rope" and "camp" first. Every question is now plain English, with the mountain only in the pictures.

## What the move to HNW changes

- **More clients each,** most of them smaller.
- **Less time each:** standard reviews done fast, AI drafts checked in bulk.
- **A different job:** deciding who to call, directing specialists, winning new clients.
- **A chance to shorten the path:** HNW work repeats more, so Associates could be certified on a standard review sooner.
- **The coaching risk:** busier Advisors coach less, and automation removes the work that got Analysts into the room.

## Decisions we need on Monday

1. **How many questions about their own path?** Today's two, or about eight. We recommend eight, asked first.
2. **One version or two?** We recommend 18 on phone, 25 on desktop. If the phone runs over eight minutes, 3.2 moves to desktop.
3. **Which bold ideas go on the cards?** We recommend all five in 5.5.
4. **Ask about classmates who didn't make it (1.5)?** We recommend yes, desktop only, hidden in groups under ten.
5. **Who supplies book sizes, and should questions name a client band** ("mostly $5m to $25m clients")? Name an owner Monday.

Build decisions we are making now: plain English everywhere, keep the AI navigation kit (3.4) with a plain question on top, add "Further ahead" to 1.4, and replace the "Freed time" card with 3.3.

## What we don't know

- **Who exactly is answering.** The brief says Associates who completed A2A, but 1.4 and 2.2 assume they are Advisors now. The class of 2025 is likely to have under a year in the role. We need each class's title and time in role.
- **Book sizes.** Current and target sizes, what "HNW" means here, and how the Private Bank and J.P. Morgan Private Client split the work.
- **Today's programme length.** Adam's brief says three years. HR to confirm.

### B. Screen-by-screen wording diagnosis

**The cause.** Respondents must learn a vocabulary before they can answer: climb, rookie, kit, pitch, own feet, crew, storm, rope, cairn. The same person is "them", "the next one", "climber", "rookie" and "Analyst". Of the 23 prompts, by our reading of the live copy (not a timed test), 5 read easily, 12 need a second read, and 6 need the metaphor decoded first or risk being misread. The fix: the question is plain English, the mountain stays in the pictures, and the person is always "the next Analyst".

| Screen | The pause | Today | Now |
|---|---|---|---|
| S01 Welcome | Never says we're redesigning A2A. Privacy line removed. | "You made the climb. Now kit out the next one." | "You made it from Analyst to Advisor. Help us make the next ones better, faster." / "Answers are held under a code, not your name, and shown only in groups of ten or more." |
| S02 Board | Four questions hidden in box labels; 12 items, 8 drops, second screen. | "Kit out the next Analyst." Do more of this · Speeds them up most · Take off their plate · Keep, but change how | 3.1 |
| F1 Classroom | A shut door reads "keep out" but means "mandatory". | "Attendance runs low. Make classroom mandatory?" / "Drag the door: shut, ajar or open." | The 4.4 follow-up |
| S04 Pace | "Them", "camps" and "when proven" all need decoding. | "Drag them up. How long should the climb to Advisor take?" / "Camps from one year to four. Or tap: when proven." | 5.2 |
| S05 AI bricks | Hardest screen: brick means AI tool, lane means timing. Jargon ("LLM chat", "watch agents"). | "Build their navigation kit. When does each piece go on?" | Dropped |
| S06 Tasks | To a banker, "pitch" means a sales pitch. Every box needs translating. | "Five pitches. Who climbs each one, and how?" On their own feet · Walk it with kit · Kit drafts, they check · Base-camp crew | 3.2 |
| S07 Cards | Prompt explains controls, not the question. "Drop it" can mean "discard the card". | "Storm coming. Four calls. Right: make it policy." | 5.5 |
| S08 Other Advisor | A what-if inside a metaphor. No "ahead" option. | "Roped to a different Advisor on day one. Where are you now?" | 1.4 |
| S10 Rating | No question, no verb. "Re-rig" is climbing jargon. | "Today's A2A route, as the next climber finds it." / "Stack stones…" | C1 |

**Sections.** Base camp is clunky: the heaviest screen comes second. Camp I (S03, S04) and the summit flow well. Camp II (S05, S06) is the clunkiest, with two AI metaphors in a row; this is probably where Adam paused. Camp III (S07 to S10) is too long and mixed: four topics and five kinds of control.

### C. HNW background and survey notes

These figures are from other firms and US advisers, not J.P. Morgan. Citi says its private bankers may serve "as few as 20" clients. US advisers serving mostly HNW clients have a median of 56 to 65. J.P. Morgan has said AI should let advisers take on about 50% more clients within three to five years. A large client takes about 62 hours a year against about 17 for a smaller one (US data). Merrill moved trainees onto teams earlier and expects 75% to graduate (the company's own figure). The human moments that stay with the Advisor: market drops, family and estate talks, big decisions.

- **Label the look-back findings.** The classes of 2022 to 2025 learned in a UHNW-weighted model, so "what made me successful" describes success in that model.
- **Questions that exist because of HNW:** 2.1, 2.2, 2.3, 3.3, 5.4 and 5.5(c).
- **Weight up what the game already has:** certifying a standard review, owning a small book, protected Advisor time, drive to win clients, bouncing back from a no.
- **Desktop option:** "What can a year-two Associate handle alone?", listing a review for a $5m client and one for a $75m family. The gap shows how far client size changes readiness, and so how far HNW could shorten the path.
- **Records, not respondents:** book size, client mix, months to Advisor, team structure. Used to read answers by who already runs a book like the future one. Reported only for groups of ten or more.
