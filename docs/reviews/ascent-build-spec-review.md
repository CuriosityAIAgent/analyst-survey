# The Ascent — spec review

Buildable, and unusually well specified for a document at this stage: the flow, the component contracts and the derived-metric table are precise enough that Claude Code could start on Monday. The instrument is not yet sound, and two of its defects are of a different order from the rest.

The first is that the study selects on its own dependent variable and never says so. Line 38 defines alumni as people who completed the programme and are "now producing"; line 95 opens with "You made the climb"; line 79 wants the link to "which alumni became top producers". Nobody who washed out of the programme, moved off the desk or left the firm is reachable by an internal SSO link, so `fuel_score` is what survivors recall, and the working group will read the "What fed the climb" bar chart (line 339) as a prescription for the next cohort.

The second is the MaxDiff design at line 46, described as "balanced" when 6 sets of 4 over 10 items can never be balanced, with `fuel_score` a raw count, so that same chart ranks items partly by how often the design showed them — and the two items feeding the honesty checks (classroom, mentor) both sit in the advantaged group.

Before anyone writes code, fix five things: the survivorship limitation and the non-response check against the HR frame, the MaxDiff design and scoring, the Level 0 cohort gate (which today blocks the 2026 intake and every ECM at screen one), the missing source for `audience`, and the colour tokens (`ink.2` on the glass layer over the summit sky measures 1.00:1 against a stated target of WCAG 2.2 AA). Three of the findings I was given did not survive my own reading and were dropped: the two claims that PixiJS has no canvas renderer (pixi.js 8.16.0+ ships `CanvasRenderer` in core and `autoDetectRenderer` has `canvas` as its terminal fallback, so this is a version pin, not a missing tier) and the claim that the phone scene reserves 38% of layout height (lines 187 and 217 put content on a glass layer *over* the scene, and line 155 already makes the year columns scroll independently).

## Ship-blockers

### Every arm selects on the outcome, and the spec makes no survivorship claim anywhere

**Severity: critical.** State the limitation before the invitations go out; add the non-response check before the first readout.

> "People who completed the analyst programme and are now producing (Associate, VP, Advisor) … they know what made them successful" (line 38)
> "You made the climb. Help us map it." (line 95)
> "which alumni became top producers, and what did they say" (line 79)
> "What fed the climb | MaxDiff scores as a ranked bar chart; alumni vs analyst vs ECM side by side" (line 339)

All three audiences are incumbents. Alumni are defined as people still producing, current analysts are people still on the programme, and ECMs are people who got promoted to manage it. An internal SSO link cannot reach anyone who left the programme, moved off the desk or left the firm, and those are precisely the people whose answer to "what fed the climb" would differ. So `fuel_score` is a ranking of what survivors remember helped, presented in a bar chart with no counterfactual arm, and the spec's own framing at line 38 ("they know what made them successful") invites the causal reading rather than warning against it.

The consequence is a decision error, not a caveat. The working group will read the top bars as the components to protect and the bottom bars as the components to cut, and the bottom bars are where survivorship bites hardest: something that eliminated people early ("time inside the plumbing", "role-plays") scores low among survivors whether it was useless or a filter. The advisor dial has the same shape — a respondent who would have failed under a different senior advisor is not in the sample, so the distribution is truncated on exactly the tail line 17 asks about, and `mentor_contradiction` becomes a candour flag on a truncated population. Nothing in the plan detects any of it: no non-response measurement, no frame count, no attrition record, and no wording separating "what fed the people who stayed" from "what should feed the next cohort".

**Fix:** three additions. (1) Add to the audiences section: "Every arm is a surviving population. The instrument measures what incumbents value and recall; it cannot measure what caused anyone to succeed, and no output may be phrased as a cause or a prescription drawn from the alumni arm alone." (2) Add a People Analytics deliverable before the first readout: a non-response check against the HR frame — invited, started and completed counts by cohort, LOB, role and (where HR holds it) leaver status, so the response profile can be compared with the frame and weighted or, at minimum, described. Leavers are the one group worth an out-of-band route: a short external-email version of Levels 1-3 for programme leavers, or an explicit statement that they were not asked. (3) Relabel the dashboard view at line 339 "What surviving producers say fed the climb", and require every fuel readout to carry the frame counts and the non-response rate on the same screen as the bars. Add to Open decisions: "- [ ] Leaver arm: field a short external version to programme leavers, or state in the readout that leavers were not reached. Recommendation: state it, and ask People Analytics for leaver counts by cohort so the size of the gap is known."

### The MaxDiff design cannot be balanced, and raw best-minus-worst is not comparable across items

**Severity: critical.** Fix before M3; unrecoverable once fielded.

> "MaxDiff sets: a balanced design of 6 sets of 4 from 10 items so each item appears at least twice; the design is fixed, not random, so scores are comparable." (line 46)
> "Design (item letters A-J): ABCD, EFGH, IJAE, BFIC, DGJH, CEHI" and "Score per item = best count minus worst count" (lines 112-113)

Four separate defects sit in those two lines.

**Balance is arithmetically impossible, not merely missed.** 6 sets × 4 slots = 24 slots over 10 items = 2.4 each. Appearances per the listed sets are A2 B2 C3 D2 E3 F2 G2 H3 I3 J2. Equal frequency requires 4*s*/10 to be an integer, so the number of sets must be a multiple of 5; 6 never can be. Taking the letters from the prose order at line 111, the four items with the extra exposure are **C** (watching how the desk works), **E** (classroom training), **H** (owning a deliverable) and **I** (the senior advisor as mentor). Because `fuel_score` is a raw count, those four have range ±3 while A, B, D, F, G, J are capped at ±2, so "What fed the climb — MaxDiff scores as a ranked bar chart" (line 339) plots two different scales on one axis. And because the design is fixed for every respondent, the bias is perfectly correlated across the sample: it does not wash out at n = 500. Note which two items got the extra shot: classroom (E) feeds `classroom_agreement`, and mentor (I) feeds `mentor_contradiction`. "The design is fixed, not random, so scores are comparable" is a non sequitur: fixing the design buys comparability *across respondents*, which is worth keeping, and nothing *across items*, which is what the bar chart needs.

**`fuel_consistency` is undefined and unreproducible.** Simulate one respondent with a strict true ranking A>B>…>J (best = first present, worst = last present in each set): A+2, B+1, C+1, D 0, E+1, F 0, G 0, H−1, I−2, J−2. B, C and E tie at +1, so "MaxDiff top-3" does not exist, and "Size of the intersection of MaxDiff top-3 and podium (0-3)" with "flag below 2" (lines 52, 315) resolves on an unspecified sort order that systematically favours the 3-appearance items. Recomputing the derived-metrics column from the same raw picks can give a different honesty verdict.

**Fourteen of the 45 item pairs are never compared by anyone.** 6 sets × C(4,2) = 36 pair slots, with 5 duplicated (BC, EH, GH, EI, CI), leaving 31 distinct pairs. The 14 never shown are AF AG AH BE BG BH BJ CG CJ DE DF DI FJ GI. **B–E is among them**: "sitting in my senior advisor's client meetings" versus "classroom training" is never traded off, and that is the comparison `classroom_agreement` exists to adjudicate. Shadowing is also never set against role-plays (B–G) or against owning a deliverable (B–H). Re-analysis cannot manufacture comparisons that were never shown.

**Position within the set is fixed too, and the straight-lining detector depends on that not being the case.** Line 112 gives four ordered letters per set and nothing shuffles them, so presentation order is identical for the whole sample and primacy loads onto the headline fuel measure in the one way a fixed design cannot average away. The same omission runs through the instrument: the podium chips (line 119), the trait list (lines 125, 132) and every follow-up chip set (lines 144-148) have no order rule either. It also breaks `satisficing_flag`, whose "same card position chosen best in 5+ rounds" (line 327) cannot separate a respondent tapping the first card from one whose favourite item keeps landing in slot 1 — and which is near-powerless at 6 rounds, firing on a given position with probability 0.005 under random responding (0.019 for any position).

**Fix:** replace line 46 with: "MaxDiff sets: 10 sets of 4 from 10 items, giving exactly 4 appearances per item, generated by a pairwise-balanced generator so every one of the 45 item pairs co-appears at least once (B–E is mandatory, since `classroom_agreement` depends on it). The set composition is fixed so scores are comparable across respondents; the position of the four cards within each set is shuffled per respondent from a seed stored on the response, and the per-appearance normalisation below is what makes scores comparable across items." Apply the same rule to the podium pool, the trait list and every follow-up chip set: composition fixed, order seeded per respondent, seed stored. Replace line 112's hand-listed sets with the generated design and publish the letter→item-id map beside it. Change line 113 and the `fuel_score` row at line 314 to "(best count minus worst count) / appearances". Redefine `fuel_consistency` tie-aware — Spearman rank correlation between the normalised MaxDiff ranking and the podium, or "count of podium items in the top tercile of normalised score" — with an explicit logged tie-break, and store `fuel_top3_tied: boolean` so the dashboard can suppress the flag when the cut was arbitrary. Restate the straight-lining leg of `satisficing_flag` as "same *displayed slot* chosen best in 7+ of 10 rounds, against the respondent's own shuffle" (P ≈ 0.0035 per slot), noting that the 10-set design recalibrates the old "5+" threshold. Add: "the choices in this design do not identify individual-level utilities; per-respondent top-3 is a flagging heuristic only, and HB estimation is aggregate-only." Change M3's criterion at line 376 from "MaxDiff design matches the fixed sets" to "MaxDiff design matches the fixed sets, every item appears exactly four times, every item pair co-appears at least once, and the within-set order differs across two seeded respondents."

**There is no shorter version of this.** Full pair coverage needs at least ⌈45/6⌉ = 8 sets and equal frequency needs a multiple of 5, so 10 is the smallest design satisfying both; 5 sets show at most 30 distinct pairs, so offering them as a fallback would contradict the requirement this fix makes mandatory. The cost has to be paid elsewhere: 6 → 10 rounds adds about 72s on the 18s-per-set costing below, on an instrument already estimated at 12.7 minutes against a 9-minute target. Take it out of the two levers named in the timing blocker — one trial card, and splitting the canvas into choose-then-place — since Level 7 at roughly 182s is where the budget actually is.

### Level 0's cohort gate locks out the 2026 intake and the entire ECM audience, and nothing in the instrument admits a non-answer

**Severity: critical.** Fix before M3. Fielded once, so an abandon at screen zero is a permanently lost respondent.

> "Inputs: cohort year (chips 2016-2025) … current role (Analyst / Associate / VP / ECM / Advisor), team size band (1-2 / 3-5 / 6+ advisors supported). All single-select." (line 96)
> "Next enabled when cohort, lob and role are set." (line 98)
> `segment: { cohort: number, lob: 'USPB'|'IPB'|'Solutions', role: string, teamBand: string }` (line 298)

The spec is dated 23 Sep 2026 and the top chip is 2025. An analyst from the 2026 intake — squarely inside "Current analysts: USPB, IPB and Solutions cohorts", and the cohort with the most to say about changed tools — has no truthful value and cannot press Next. Worse, the same screen offers **ECM** as a role while line 40 defines that audience as "Managers of analysts", many of whom never went through the programme: cohort year is not a well-defined attribute for them, there is no "not applicable" chip, `cohort` is a non-nullable `number`, and there is no "prefer not to say" or skip anywhere in the instrument. Every ECM either abandons at screen zero or invents a cohort, which then contaminates the two dashboard views that cut on cohort ("The readiness curve", "Mark to market") and the global segmentation at line 334. Line 398 recommends fielding all three audiences in the same week, so two of the three are blocked on day one.

Separately, `teamBand` is a required `string` in the contract but is absent from the Next gate, and the bands are "advisors supported" with no zero option — an Advisor or an ECM supporting no advisors can legitimately reach Next with it unset and fail zod on POST, breaking M3's own criterion "Response JSON for levels 0-6 validates against the zod schema."

The cohort chip is the visible instance of a policy gap that runs the length of the instrument. Every field in the Response record is non-optional, every Next gate requires an answer, and no level offers a skip, a "prefer not to say", a "don't know" or an "it depends". All five trials force a binary on policy questions where "it depends" is the honest ECM answer, and the branch then demands a follow-up on a position the respondent may not hold — so the yes/no split that line 343 makes the headline of "The bold questions" is partly manufactured. Forced-choice is defensible on the MaxDiff, where the trade-off *is* the measurement; it is not defensible on "Would you make classroom training mandatory?", and a survey with no non-response option produces fabrication rather than completeness.

**Fix:** replace line 96's cohort clause with: "cohort year (chips: '2015 or earlier', 2016 … current year, 'I didn't come through the analyst programme'); the range is derived from a single content constant (min year, max = current year), with a unit test asserting the max chip year is not below the current year." Add "0 / none" to the team-size band. Replace line 98 with: "Next enabled when lob and role are set. Cohort is required for the alumni and analyst audiences and optional for ECM." Widen line 298 to `segment: { cohort: number | 'pre2016' | 'na' | null, lob: 'USPB'|'IPB'|'Solutions', role: string, teamBand: string | null }` and add: "every dashboard view that segments by cohort declares its unknown-cohort bucket rather than dropping rows silently."

Then write the missingness policy once, in the design principles, and hold every level to it: "Any item may be left unanswered. Levels 1, 5 and 8 carry a visible skip; the trials carry a third option, 'it depends — and here's what on', with the same follow-up chips offered to both branches; the MaxDiff and the podium are the two forced-choice mechanics and say so on screen. Every field that can be skipped is optional in the zod contract, every derived metric states its behaviour on a null, and the dashboard reports an answered count per item rather than treating a skip as a zero." Add `trials[].answer: 'yes' | 'no' | 'depends'` and report the three-way split in the view at line 342.

### `audience` is in the API contract and nothing in the spec sets it

**Severity: critical.** Fix before M3. Irrecoverable: the wording a respondent saw cannot be un-asked.

> `audience: 'alumni' | 'analyst' | 'ecm'` (line 296)
> Level 0 data list: "`segment.cohort`, `segment.lob`, `segment.role`, `segment.teamBand`, `variant.canvasArm`, `variant.trialOrder`" (line 97)

`audience` is the only field in the Response record with no specified source. Every other field has one: `token` from the token service (line 263), `variant.canvasArm` from a logged 50/50 draw, `variant.trialOrder` from a logged randomisation, the segments from Level 0. Yet `audience` drives "copy variants per audience" (line 362), "labels vary by audience" (line 383), M3's "audience variants" (line 376) and the headline "alumni vs analyst vs ECM side by side" (line 339). It cannot be derived from `segment.role`, because line 38 puts **Advisor** inside the alumni audience while line 40 puts "senior advisors" inside the ECM audience, and because the Base camp copy must already be in the right tense on the screen where role is asked — line 95's "You made the climb" is the alumni past-tense variant.

The concrete failure: the front end is a static export with no variant selector specified, so a builder hard-codes one default, and the only fully drafted copy in the spec is alumni-toned. Current analysts and ECMs answer past-tense stems, and the cross-audience gap the study exists to measure becomes a wording artefact.

**Fix:** add to line 97: "`audience` (set from the invitation token before first paint, never from the role chip, immutable thereafter)". Add a sentence under governance: "The token service resolves `audience` from the invitation list; a request with no resolvable audience is refused rather than defaulted. Both `audience` and `segment.role` are persisted; a respondent whose audience and role disagree (an alumnus now managing analysts) is routed to the ECM instrument, analysed as a separate stratum, and flagged `audience_role_mismatch`." Add to M3's criterion: "the same URL under three token audiences renders three different Base camp headlines and three different Level 2 stems."

### The ECM instrument cannot be completed truthfully, and the three audiences share no anchor item

**Severity: critical.** Fix before M3. Fielded once, on what line 34 calls "the headline output".

> Alumni "Past tense: 'what fed your climb'" versus ECMs "Third person: 'what should feed an analyst's climb'", and "the gaps between them are the headline output" (lines 34-40)
> "Alumni wording uses past tense; ECM wording asks 'which teaches an analyst most'." (line 114)

Two defects, and the second is what makes this a blocker rather than an analysis note: a laterally hired ECM cannot truthfully complete Levels 1, 4 and 5, and `readiness.month`, `dna.sort` and `dna.advisorDial` are all non-optional, so a third of the instrument either forces invention or cannot be finished at all.

The do-versus-should contrast is the intent, so the defect is not a conflation, it is the absence of a decomposable anchor: no item is asked in identical framing to all three audiences, so an alumni-minus-ECM difference in `fuel_score` cannot be split into value disagreement versus the descriptive/normative frame and memory availability. Alumni put "the morning meeting" high because they sat in it 700 times; ECMs put it low because they would prescribe role-plays; the working group reads "managers don't value what made analysts successful" off a chart that would look identical if both groups held the same values. Line 114 is also internally inconsistent with line 40: the audiences table gives the ECM frame as prescriptive ("should feed"), while the only level that implements a variant asks "which teaches an analyst most", a causal-efficacy judgement, which is a third construct.

Below Level 2 it gets worse. Levels 1, 4 and 5 are first-person autobiographical ("When did you feel ready to run?", "Born with it / Built it here / Still building", "If you'd been tagged to a different senior advisor on day one"), and line 114 is the spec's only ECM wording. A laterally hired ECM cannot answer them, yet `readiness.month`, `dna.sort` and `dna.advisorDial` are non-optional, and `compress_contradiction` and `mentor_contradiction` are computed uniformly, so the Data quality view manufactures false dishonesty flags for the ECM stratum by comparing a policy prescription against a personal fact.

**Fix:** make the prescriptive item ("what should feed the next cohort's climb") the one measure fielded to all three audiences and the only thing plotted side by side; keep the retrospective item as an alumni/analyst extra plotted separately, and label each dashboard column with the framing it was asked in. Reconcile line 114 with line 40 (pick "should feed" or "teaches most", not both). Specify a per-level wording variant or an explicit ECM skip for all ten levels in `src/content`, with Levels 1, 4 and 5 skipped for ECMs by default, made visible in the schema as optional fields and in the flow machine. Gate every derived metric on audience so readiness, DNA and contradiction metrics are not computed for the ECM instrument.

### Five trial asides prompt the answer, and three of them feed the honesty checks

**Severity: high.** Fix before M3. Fielded once: a prompted answer cannot be un-prompted, and the trials are the spec's headline output.

> "| classroom | Would you make classroom training mandatory? | Be honest. Attendance says otherwise. |" (line 148)
> "| certify | … | Pilots are. Surgeons are. |" (line 144)
> "| compress | … | Schools already let students run at their own pace. |" (line 146)
> "| agents | Three years from now you'll have agents working for you. Do you see yourself running a team of them? | A manager at 24, of software. |" (line 147)
> "one wink per screen at most ('Be honest. Attendance says otherwise.')" (line 215)

These are not winks, they are prompts for a specific answer, and three of them sit inside items the spec uses to judge respondent honesty. "Be honest. Attendance says otherwise" states the answer as known fact before the respondent gives it, so a respondent with classroom in their MaxDiff top three who is nudged to "no" loses a point of `classroom_agreement` and the demand characteristic manufactures the inconsistency the metric reports. "Pilots are. Surgeons are." supplies two maximally authoritative precedents for yes, so the certify split is not readable as a level of support and the between-audience gap the spec calls "the headline output" is compressed by a ceiling effect rather than measured. The agents card presupposes its own premise ("Three years from now you'll have agents working for you"), so dissent requires contradicting the instrument, and the no-branch offers only worry-coded options, unlike the avatars no-branch which at least includes "honestly nothing". Line 104's "When did you feel ready to run? Not when we let you, when you knew" pulls `readiness.month` earlier, straight into `readiness_gap = 36 − month` and the `readiness.month <= 18` gate.

Note the direction problem, which is worse than a one-way bias: on `compress_contradiction` the Level 1 stem pushes more respondents *inside* the ≤18 gate while the compress aside pushes them *out of* the `no` arm, so the net sign is indeterminate and cannot be corrected analytically. The worst offender is also the spec's own exemplar of house voice at line 215.

**Fix:** replace line 215's example with the avatars aside ("Practise the pitch without a real client's face falling"), which sets scene without prescribing an answer, and add the rule: "An aside may set scene or tone. It must not assert a fact about the respondent's behaviour, supply a precedent for one answer, or presuppose the stem." Delete the classroom and compress asides, or render them only after the answer locks. Soften certify's aside. Rewrite the agents stem to "Some expect analysts to be running AI agents within three years. Do you see yourself doing that?" and add a neutral option to its no-branch. Change Level 1's stem to "When did you feel ready to run a client review alone?" and drop "Not when we let you". Add wording neutrality as an explicit criterion of the working-group copy review at line 389.

### "Optional voice input … transcribed on device" is false on both target platforms

**Severity: critical.** Cut before M2, because the `voice` prop lands with the component library, weeks before the DUC notice that was meant to sanction it.

> "Optional voice input on mobile (Web Speech API), transcribed on device." (line 165)
> "| TextField | Multiline with optional voice input | value, onChange, voice | standard |" (line 243)
> "Safari iOS 16+ and Chrome Android" (line 285)

On iOS every browser is WebKit, and WebKit's `SpeechRecognition` streams audio to Apple's speech service with no on-device control exposed to the page. Chrome's local path (`SpeechRecognition.available({ processLocally: true })` / `install()`) is opt-in, needs a downloaded language pack, and is not available on Chrome Android. So on the two platforms line 285 names, "transcribed on device" cannot be requested at all, let alone guaranteed. A correct feature-detect gate would hide the mic on 100% of the mobile fleet the spec offers it to.

The consequence is not a browser-trivia correction, it is a false assurance inside the document line 77 calls "the version to take to DUC". A VP alumna dictating "my ECM held me back for three years and the desk knows it" into "The one thing you'd change tomorrow" sends raw employee audio — the least redactable field in the instrument, on the screen the governance table singles out for separate storage and review — to a consumer cloud service outside any vendor agreement. That falsifies line 28 ("Nothing is collected that the intro does not describe") and line 77 ("Your answers are held by People Analytics under a code"). The spec already handles the identical problem correctly one section later: the AI interviewer goes to "the firm's approved LLM endpoint … behind a feature flag; ship without it if approvals lag" (line 283), with a matching Open decision. Voice sends a more sensitive payload and got a reassurance instead of a gate. It also breaks line 225's own rule: "no feature exists on one breakpoint only; the same answer is captured the same way on every device."

**Fix:** delete the sentence at line 165. Delete `voice` from the TextField props at line 243. Add to Open decisions: "- [ ] Voice capture at Level 8: drop for v1, or route to an internal ASR endpoint in v2. Depends on endpoint approval, same as the AI interviewer. Recommendation: drop for v1." If it is kept against that recommendation, add to line 379: "egress and Content-Security-Policy review of the speech endpoint" and add `source: 'typed' | 'asr:<processor>'` to `mark.change` / `mark.missing` so the dataset records which path produced each answer.

### The dashboard segments 270 cells with no minimum cell size

**Severity: high.** Fix in M6, but state the rule now so it lands in the DUC submission.

> "The dashboard answers Adam's questions directly, segmented by audience, cohort, business and team band." (line 334)
> "| Data quality | Consistency index distribution, contradiction flags, satisficing flags, completion time | derived |" (line 345)
> "The working group sees patterns, not people." (line 77)

Audience × cohort × business × team band = 3 × 10 × 3 × 3 = 270 cells (the collected segment space is 10 × 3 × 5 × 3 = 450). No minimum cell size, suppression rule or aggregation floor appears anywhere in the 406 lines: not in the governance table, not in the conventions, not in Open decisions, not in M6's acceptance criteria. "Pen-test checklist passed" cannot catch it, so nothing in the plan forces the question before launch.

Cohort × role is structurally sparse — a 2016-cohort Analyst, a 2024-cohort VP, a Solutions ECM in the 6+ band — so the tail contains singletons at any sample size this programme can produce. A working-group member who filters to "IPB / 2019 / VP / 6+ advisors" and sees n = 1 is reading that person's `mark.score`, their `advisorDial`, their readiness month and their `satisficing_flag`, in a room of people who know the cohort. The Data quality view is the worst case, because contradiction and satisficing flags are individual-level by construction: at n = 1 it labels a named-by-filter individual a satisficer. The row-level export compounds it — "a de-identified CSV per level" (line 347) is de-identified only in the SID sense, and carries the four segment columns per row, so anyone with export rights reconstructs every cell in Excel regardless of what the dashboard suppresses.

M6's acceptance criterion currently *requires* the defect: "a real device completes the game and its row appears in the dashboard with all derived metrics."

**Fix:** add a governance row: "| Disclosure | Unlimited filtering | No tile, view or export resolves below k = 5 respondents; the API returns a `suppressed` marker rather than rows, and complementary cells are suppressed to block differencing. Cohort is offered to the dashboard in bands (2016-18 / 2019-21 / 2022-24 / 2025+), with the year retained in the People Analytics extract for the readiness curve. Free-text views carry no segment filter beyond audience. The Data quality view is unsegmented. |" Enforce it in the query layer both the dashboard and the CSV export call, not in the React view. Strip segment columns from the per-level CSVs at line 347 and export banded aggregates plus an unsegmented row-level file. Rewrite M6's criterion at line 379 to: "a real device completes the game, its derived metrics are present in the database, and a single-respondent filter in the dashboard returns suppression rather than a row." State k in the DUC submission, and add to Open decisions: "- [ ] Minimum reporting cell size (k) and cohort banding for the dashboard. Recommendation: k = 5, cohort in 3-year bands."

### `GET /responses/:token` has no caller authentication, and the token is specified two incompatible ways

**Severity: high.** Fix before M1, because M1 ships the magic-link token path.

> "| API | one POST /responses/:token/levels/:id, one GET /responses/:token | Small surface, easy to review |" (line 275)
> "| Auth | SSO redirect issues a signed session token; demo mode uses a shareable magic link with a throwaway token | Same code path for demo and production |" (line 277)
> "the token service issues an opaque token per session and People Analytics holds the salt" (line 263)
> "a session started on desktop resumes on phone via the same token" (line 374)

Two problems, one root cause.

**The token is defined three ways and only one satisfies M1.** Line 263 says "per session"; line 71 derives it as a salted hash of the SID, which is deterministic per person and therefore permanent; line 374 requires the same token to survive two independent SSO authentications on two devices. A per-session token fails M1's own test; a salted-SID token is a stable lifetime pseudonym. Whichever you choose, say which, because the deterministic option means a second wave next year joins to this one on the same key.

**Possession of the token is full read and write access.** "Token verification" on a path parameter is circular: verifying that the token was validly minted says nothing about who is calling. There is no cookie, no `Authorization` header, no binding to the SSO subject, no expiry, no revocation, and the words "rate limit" and "TLS" do not appear in the document. A token in a URL path lands in browser history, the corporate TLS-terminating proxy's access logs, a shared-screen call, and any forwarded link. `GET` then returns `mark.change`, `mark.missing`, `dna.advisorDial` and `trials`, i.e. "the advisor didn't matter" and a 3/10 grade. The same credential model on `POST` is worse in one respect: it grants blind write access, so a record can be overwritten with no audit trail (the string "audit" appears nowhere in the spec) and `satisficing_flag` will not catch a plausible forgery. Line 277's rationale, "Same code path for demo and production", is what propagates shareable-link semantics into production, and M6's only security gate is one unqualified bullet.

**Fix:** replace line 275 with: "`POST /responses/me/levels/:id`, `GET /responses/me`; the server resolves the pseudonym from the SSO session, not from the URL." Replace line 277's reason column with: "Production resolves identity from an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax session cookie set on the SSO redirect; the response key is a stable per-respondent pseudonym derived from the SID by a keyed HMAC whose key is held in People Analytics' KMS, scoped per study so waves do not join without an explicit re-linking step. The demo magic-link token is single-use, exchanged for a cookie on first load, and is scoped to seeded demo records only — it can never address a production response." ### The build canvas's capacity never binds globally, duplicate placement is unspecified, and the custom brick has no coding plan

**Severity: medium.**

> "the respondent designs the next cohort's three years under a capacity constraint. Sequencing, omissions and gate placement are the data" (line 154)
> "three columns (Year 1, 2, 3), each a capacity of 10 units" (line 155)
> "20-brick scripted session produces the expected events and derived metrics" (line 377)

The palette is 15 bricks totalling 1+2+1+2+1+1+3+2+1+2+2+1+1+1+1 = 22 units against 3 × 10 = 30, so 8 units of slack. Line 399 tells the programme team to swap in the real bricks and says nothing about re-deriving capacity, so the constraint can be silently un-bound by a content change.

Whether a brick can be placed twice is never stated. `build.years[3][]` of `{brickId, label?, position}` has no instance identity, which leans single-use, but `build_delta`'s added/removed/moved-earlier/moved-later taxonomy is brickId-keyed and becomes ill-defined if the same id appears twice — and `custom` is the brick most likely to be wanted more than once.

The view at line 343 wants "the most common removed and added bricks", which cannot aggregate free-text labels; `build_mix` then reports a "custom" share that means "something the palette was missing" without saying what, which is the most interesting answer on the screen and the only one the dashboard cannot report. The visual stud height is 44px per unit, but a placed brick of any size is a single drag target of at least 44px." Restate M4's criterion as an actual script: "n places, m over-capacity attempts refused, k moves, j removes produce the expected event sequence and the expected `build_units`, `build_mix`, `gate_first_year`, `year3_empty` and `build_delta` values." For the custom brick: cap it at two instances with distinct instance ids, keep its label in the free-text store with the same review gate as `mark.change`, exclude it from `build_mix`'s named shares and report it as a separate "gaps in the palette" count plus a coded list produced in the same review pass, and state that a custom label is never joined to a segment filter below the k floor.

### `readiness.month` mixes lived, censored, remembered and normative months in one column

**Severity: medium.**

> "a flag the respondent drags along a 36-month track … Months 1-36" and "When did you feel ready to run?" (lines 103-104)
> "| readiness_gap | 36 minus readiness.month |" (line 318)
> "All enum values are stable string ids (never labels) so wording variants across audiences map to the same columns" (line 330)

Every current analyst is censored by construction, because anyone past 36 months is an alumnus. With cohort chips capped at 2025 and today 24 Sep 2026, a 2025-cohort analyst has roughly 14 months of tenure and a 2024-cohort analyst roughly 26, so their `readiness_gap` is forced to ≥22 and ≥10 before they express any preference. Line 39 sells exactly this comparison as "Mike's baseline; comparison against alumni shows what has changed", so the readiness curve will report censoring as cohort change — and in the direction that reads as evidence for compressing the programme, which is the study's biggest decision. It cannot be fixed in analysis: `segment.cohort` is a year, so tenure is known only to ±12 months and no censoring adjustment is available from what is stored. `compress_contradiction` (month ≤ 18 and compress = no) also fires near-automatically for current analysts whose tenure caps them under 18 months, so the consistency index penalises one audience for its tenure. There is no way to record "I don't feel ready yet", and an ECM answering about an analyst has no lived month at all.

Recall error runs the other way and lands in the same column. A 2016-cohort alumna is asked to place a flag on one month out of 36 for an internal feeling up to ten years old, with month-level ticks and a haptic tick per month implying a precision she cannot have, no "I don't remember" state and no confidence capture; a 2025-cohort analyst is answering about last year. The readiness curve at line 337 plots those cohorts against each other by construction, so recall precision varies systematically with the axis the chart reads as change over time — and it degrades in the same direction as the censoring already flagged, towards later remembered months for older cohorts. Two errors of known sign, on the study's biggest decision, both invisible in what is stored.

**Fix:** add `readiness.notYet: boolean` with a visible "not yet, still climbing" end state on the track, cap the track at estimated tenure for the analyst audience with a marker for where they are now, and add `segment.tenureMonths` (or an intake month, not just a year). Store the ECM answer as `readiness.shouldMonth` in a distinct field so it is never unioned with lived months. Exclude `notYet` and ECM responses from `compress_contradiction`. Scope `readiness_gap` to alumni and label it audience-scoped, or drop it and report `readiness.month` directly — as a decreasing affine bijection of the month it adds no information while asserting a 36-month standard the compress trial is asking respondents to question. Default the readiness curve to complete cases and show censored respondents as a separate labelled series. Snap the track to quarters beyond month 18 for alumni more than five years out, capture `readiness.confidence` as a three-point chip under the flag ("I remember clearly / roughly / honestly guessing"), and label the readiness curve with the median cohort age of each series so the recall gradient is visible in the readout rather than buried in it.

### The 50/50 canvas arm has no stated sample size or minimum detectable effect

## Worth a ticket

1. **Pin `pixi.js >= 8.16.0`** in the stack table. The canvas renderer was removed in 8.0 and reintroduced into core in 8.16.0, so "PixiJS (WebGL, canvas fallback)" is accurate only above that version. Set `AbstractRenderer.defaultOptions.failIfMajorPerformanceCaveat = true` so software-rendered WebGL on a managed or VDI desktop falls through to canvas rather than running at single-digit fps, and add a mid-session downgrade on sustained frame drops.
2. **Name the firm's desktop browser and version.** Line 285 calls it "primary" and never identifies it. Neither stated gate can be measured on it: Lighthouse is Chrome-only and Playwright does not drive a managed VDI build. Replace "60fps on … a corporate laptop in the firm's desktop browser" with a named machine plus "median frame time ≤ 16.7ms and p95 ≤ 22ms over a 30s scripted walkthrough", and add a `--use-gl=swiftshader` run asserting the canvas path is selected and the scene is not blank. The iPhone target is stated two ways and the weaker one is in the acceptance criterion: line 213 says "a three-year-old iPhone", line 378 says "iPhone 12 in Safari", a six-year-old device as of the spec's own date. Name one in both places — the oldest iPhone still receiving iOS updates at fielding. Add 360px to the Playwright matrix too: line 285 supports 360px upwards and M1 tests only 1440px, iPad, iPhone 14 and Pixel 7.
3. **Name the buckets in the JS budget.** "total JS under 400KB gzipped excluding the scene; scene assets under 1.5MB" leaves the PixiJS runtime, the two variable fonts and the 12 SVG illustrations unowned. Replace with: app JS ≤ 180KB gz, vendor JS ≤ 150KB gz, PixiJS ≤ 200KB gz loaded after first interaction and gated separately, fonts ≤ 130KB, scene textures ≤ 1.2MB, each enforced by `size-limit` as a merge gate. Note `zod` classic does not tree-shake through `z.*`; use `zod/mini` on the client or keep validation server-side. Change the paint target from "first paint under 1.5s on 4G" to LCP ≤ 2.5s and TBT ≤ 200ms, since prerendered markup can hit first paint while the screen is unusable.
4. **Give the Response record a flow position.** There is no `progress` field, so cross-device resume must infer position from which answer objects are populated, which returns a respondent who backed up from Level 8 to Level 3 to Level 8 mid-edit, contradicting "Back is always available; nothing is lost on back". Add `progress: { levelId, subIndex, maxReached, updatedAt }`, write it on every `screen_exit`, and state that the machine is hydrated from `progress`, never from answer presence. Add a Playwright case: forward to Level 8, back to Level 3, reload, assert Level 3.
5. **Make per-level writes versioned and idempotent.** `POST` carries `{ clientId, revision, clientUpdatedAt }`; the server rejects a lower revision for that level and returns the winning document. Without it, a stale desktop tab or a late offline flush silently overwrites a fresher answer with no way to tell afterwards.
6. **Specify the IndexedDB lifecycle.** Delete the local record on successful final POST and whenever `completedAt` is set, expire it after 72 hours regardless, and add a "finish and clear this device" action on the Summit. Today the full candid record plus the token persists in the browser profile of a shared meeting-room desktop indefinitely, which is also the answer DUC will want on where data rests.
7. **Add security headers as host requirements, not app requirements.** `output: 'export'` silently ignores a `headers()` block in `next.config.js`, so Content-Security-Policy (no inline script, no third-party font or asset CDNs — self-host Fraunces and Plus Jakarta Sans), `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, HSTS and a CORS allowlist of exactly the game origin must be written as configuration requirements on "the JPM internal static host", with an owner. Note this collides with the Qualtrics iframe option, which needs a permissive `frame-ancestors`.
8. **Correct the Qualtrics fallback to distribution only.** Line 281 offers "via an iframe wrapper" while line 403 recommends "Qualtrics only for distribution if required". Embedding is a different auth architecture: the SSO redirect cannot run in a cross-origin frame, `allow="gyroscope; microphone"` belongs to the Qualtrics page so tilt parallax and voice silently no-op, and `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` is 0 in a nested browsing context so the pinned FooterBar sits under the iPhone home bar. Delete "via an iframe wrapper" and state that the Qualtrics item is a link out carrying the token.
9. **Treat free text as data in the LLM clustering step.** "themed free text (LLM-clustered, quotes de-identified)" never names the endpoint or defines "de-identified". Pass respondent text in a separate delimited channel from the analysis instructions, one item per call; route it to the same approved endpoint as the interviewer behind the same flag; run a deterministic pre-pass (SID regex, HR name dictionary, email and phone patterns) before any model call; and keep the People Analytics review on the raw text, not on the model's summary, since the reviewer's only view of the corpus is otherwise the model's output.

Put numerals on the fill bar ("Year 1 · 8/10 units"), write one refusal line into the existing live hint line for 4s in an `aria-live="polite"` region ("Own a small book of names needs 3 units; Year 1 has 2 left"), and override dnd-kit's default announcement, which currently reports a refused drop as a successful one.
