# The Ascent: the adaptive flow

**The idea.** Every card asks for one thumb-move about something the graduate actually went through: a tap, a swipe, a token or a short drag. There are no grids and no percentages. The next card depends on the last answer and often quotes it back ("Leading the conversation. What built that?", "Blue on Lead the relationship…"), so it reads like a senior colleague following up rather than a form. People answer a follow-up more honestly than a questionnaire, and each follow-up only reaches the people it applies to, so nobody is asked something that isn't about them.

The graph is in `src/content/flow.json`: 87 cards, and 30 of them on the main path.

## Where it goes deeper

- If you mark A2A 3 or below on the prep scale, we ask where it fell short. At 7 or above, we ask what it got right.
- If you put a block on classroom, we ask whether classroom should be mandatory.
  - If you say yes, we ask "even if it cost a client meeting that day?"
  - If you say no, we ask what would work instead.
  - If you say it depends, we ask what it depends on.
- Whatever ability you say clicked, the "what built it" rank quotes that ability back to you. There are seven versions of the card.
- Whatever you rank top on that card, the "what let you absorb it" card names it. There are five versions.
- If you say "it stung, then it stuck", we ask what stung.
- If you say someone explained the why, we ask whether that was built into the programme or luck of the team.
- If you say repetition or using it straight away, we ask roughly how many goes it took.
- If you answered "Not there yet" at the start of Hindsight, we ask what would get you there sooner.
- If you say it could have happened earlier (a year or more, or a bit), we ask what held it back. If you say it depends, we ask what on.
- If you swipe "arrived with it" on reading people or on a hunter's drive, we ask whether A2A can teach it to others.
- Your blue token decides which "which experience, exactly?" card you get. There are six versions.
- If you place a red token, we ask what every Analyst should spend less time on in that area.
  - If the same area also has green or blue, we ask what splits it.
  - Then we ask how they should be freed from it.
  - If the answer is reduce, automate or reassign, we ask what the Analyst would stop learning.
- If "presented in a client meeting" is one of your three, we ask how many times it takes before it counts.
- If you say it depends on the avatar question, we ask what it would hinge on.
- If you say yes to certification, we ask how someone should prove they're ready, then how you'd feel about mandatory AI-avatar role plays. If you say it depends, we ask what on.
- If you say it depends on the different-Advisor question, we ask what it would have come down to.
- If you felt ready at two years or sooner, we ask "ready to do what?" instead of "what took the time?"
- If you say it depends on pacing, we ask what pacing hinges on.
- If you hand any task to AI on the swipe, we ask what the Analyst would stop learning, then which tasks they should do alone before seeing AI output.
- If you ranked "doing the prep myself" top and then hand the pack, brief or research to AI, we ask "Doing the prep built you. How will the next Analyst learn it?"
- If you say it depends on the five-year question, we ask what the future role depends on.
- If you picked "Something not here" anywhere, we ask you to name what our lists missed. This is optional.
- The one-change card lists your blue area as one of its options, unmarked.

## How we catch staging

- "Doing the prep myself" ranked first, then prep handed to AI. The follow-up quotes the rank back, and "They won't need to" is a fair way out.
- A task swiped "Mostly grew me", then swiped "AI leads, Analyst reviews". This one is never quoted back.
- "Effective in year one", then "Needed more than three years" on the timing scale, asked in a different section and format.
- Senior Advisor ranked first on what readiness depended on, then "Yes, mostly my own doing" on the different-Advisor question.
- Blue token placed on an area, then that area not chosen as the one change when it appears unprompted.

The trait rank against the arrived-or-built swipe tests Adam's EQ hypothesis. It never flags anyone. Three further pairs are read side by side for context and aren't flags either: classroom blocks against mandatory classroom, blocks against what built the ability, and "could it have happened earlier" against skills-based pacing.

## What we deliberately did not ask

**Current-state time.** Two cards cover it. The Analyst-work swipe (card 13) asks whether each of six real tasks mostly served the client or mostly grew them. The red token, with its "spend less time on" follow-up, asks where time is wasted. We stopped there. Graduates recalling a time split from years ago would give us precise-looking noise, and asking for percentages is the grid we set out to avoid. What we need from them is the judgement of which work built them, not a timesheet.

**Left to the in-person session:**
- **The four-level AI ladder as a scale.** We rebuild it per task from the swipe, the "do it alone first" card and the red follow-ups. The rungs are never shown, because a visible ladder invites people to pick the middle. The room can test whether the levels mean the same thing to everyone.
- **"What they should get more of" as a sentence.** This is stem 1. It comes from the green tokens and is replayed on the closing screen. The room is a better place to put it into words.
- **Anything about named people or teams.** The survey asks about roles and the system throughout. Specific Advisors and teams stay in the room, where context can be given and nothing is written down against a name.
- **Why people say "It depends".** Each "It depends" costs one extra tap, and some people will learn to avoid it. The room gives us a rate to compare against.
